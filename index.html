    01 · Sensor base

    Printed sensor base

    The build starts at the bottom. This printed plate replaces the old shell bottom and carries all three downward-looking sensors, so they see the ground during the descent.

    3D printedØ66 mm
    01 · Sensor base

    PETG window

    A thin PETG disc drops in over the camera opening, protecting the lens while letting it look straight down through the base.

    16 × 0.5 mmPETG
    01 · Sensor base

    Pi Camera Module 2 NoIR

    A camera with no infrared-blocking filter, fitted with a blue gel. It sees near-infrared and blue light at once, the classic setup for NDVI-style vegetation imaging. Four self-tapping screws hold it down.

    SC00244 × M2×6
    01 · Sensor base

    MLX90640 thermal camera

    A 32 × 24 pixel thermal imager. Plastic blocks thermal infrared, so this one looks through an open hole instead of a window. A printed clamp bar and two screws hold it in place.

    Pimoroni PIM36519 × 22 mm55° FOV2 × M2×8
    01 · Sensor base

    AS7263 NIR spectrometer

    The third sensor measures six near-infrared bands, adding spectral readings to the two images. Two M3 screws finish the sensor base.

    SparkFun SEN-1435125.4 × 25.4 mm2 × M3×8
    02 · Sled

    The sled

    The backbone of the electronics. The Raspberry Pi mounts on one side and the battery on the other, and both PCBs slide down into it later.

    3D printed
    02 · Sled

    Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W

    The payload computer, with its microSD card already fitted. It goes in from inside the sled onto four posts, held by self-tapping screws with their heads on the Pi.

    4 × M2.5×4microSD
    02 · Sled

    18650 battery holder

    The holder sits between the sled's guide rails, on the opposite face from the Pi.

    LX321477.5 × 21 × 21.5 mm
    02 · Sled

    Samsung 30Q cell

    One 18650 lithium-ion cell powers the whole can. Two cable ties wrap around the holder and cell so nothing shifts on landing.

    INR18650-30QØ18.33 × 64.85 mm2 × cable tie
    03 · PCB B, flight computer

    Flight computer board

    A XIAO ESP32S3 microcontroller and a microSD logger sit on headers. Beside them, a BMP280 pressure sensor for altitude, and the Ra-02 LoRa radio soldered straight onto the board for the 433 MHz telemetry link.

    42 × 77.5 mmESP32-S3BMP280LoRa Ra-02
    03 · PCB B, flight computer

    PCB B slides in

    The finished board slides down into the sled, right next to the Pi.

    03 · PCB A, power & interface

    Power and interface board

    A TP4056 USB-C charger, a pushbutton power switch, two 5 V regulators (one for the main electronics, one for the Pi), the GPS receiver and a 12 mm buzzer, all on 2.5 mm headers.

    52 × 77.5 mmTP40562 × U3V40F5NEO-6M
    03 · PCB A, power & interface

    PCB A slides in

    The power board slides down on the battery side of the sled. Both boards now stand upright inside it.

    04 · Antenna deck

    Antenna deck and SMA jack

    The deck caps the sled. An SMA bulkhead jack goes up through the 6.6 mm hole in its tower and is locked with a washer and nut. The radio antenna will screw onto it at the very end.

    SMA bulkhead8 mm hex
    04 · Antenna deck

    GPS patch antenna

    An active patch antenna lies flat on the deck plate, facing the sky through the top of the can.

    25 × 25 × 7.8 mmactive
    04 · Antenna deck

    Deck onto the sled

    The deck drops onto the sled and two self-tapping screws hold it. Later the top cap clamps it down as well.

    2 × M2×8
    05 · Shell

    Grated shell body

    The body comes down over the sensor base and twists to lock its bayonet. The gratings keep the can light and let air reach the sensors.

    bayonet lock3D printed
    05 · Shell

    Sled stack goes in

    The whole sled stands up and lowers through the 59.4 mm top opening until it rests on the internal ledge. The shell turns see-through so you can follow it.

    59.4 mm opening
    06 · Close

    Threaded top cap

    The cap screws onto the body, closing the can and clamping the antenna deck in place.

    06 · Close

    433 MHz antenna

    The last part. The antenna screws onto the SMA jack, and its tip ends 39 mm above the can, inside the 45 mm limit.

    TX433-JZ-4SMA-J, 40 mm
